# Study Method, Progress and Safety

## Weekly budget: 10 hours
- 2h structured course.
- 1h official documentation.
- 4h hands-on labs.
- 2h project work.
- 1h review and self-test.

Adapt days around university commitments. Consistency and evidence matter more than a rigid calendar.

## Learning loop
1. Learn the concept and vocabulary.
2. Reproduce a guided example.
3. Repeat without copying.
4. Apply it to a mini-project.
5. Explain it in your own words.
6. Review mistakes and update notes.

## AI use
- Ask for explanations and hints, not blind answers.
- Attempt labs independently before requesting help.
- Never submit code you cannot explain.
- Verify AI suggestions against official documentation.
- Never paste secrets, credentials, private logs or personal data into AI tools.

## Evidence of completion
Keep notes, commands/configuration, lab objective and scope, sanitized screenshots/logs, failures and fixes, a knowledge check, and a project/report link.

## Weekly self-test
Can you explain the topic without notes? Repeat the lab from a clean environment? Troubleshoot a variation? Explain security implications and mitigations? If not, review before moving on.

## Safety and ethics
- Test only systems you own or have explicit written permission to assess.
- Use isolated virtual networks and intentionally vulnerable apps such as Juice Shop.
- Do not scan public IP ranges, attack real accounts, bypass access controls, or test third-party services without authorization.
- Keep malware analysis isolated; never run unknown samples on personal/production devices.
- Sanitize reports, screenshots, PCAPs and logs before publishing.
- Follow responsible disclosure and platform rules of engagement.

Course availability, free tiers, exam objectives, prices and prerequisites change. Check official provider pages before enrolling or paying. A certificate does not replace practical skill or experience.
